### Punctuation Preference: Em Dashes, Ellipses, and Parentheses

Treat em dashes as a specialized tool rather than a default punctuation mark. AI writing tends to overuse em dashes as a catch-all solution for pauses, interruptions, exposition, emphasis, and transitions. Avoid this habit.

Before using an em dash, consider whether a comma, period, colon, semicolon, parentheses, ellipsis, or simple sentence restructuring would accomplish the same purpose more naturally. If another option works as well or better, use it.

For interrupted dialogue, prefer an ellipsis over an em dash. This is a deliberate stylistic choice and should be followed consistently.

Example:

"Wait, I thought you said..."

instead of

"Wait, I thought you said—"

For exposition, asides, clarifications, and authorial commentary, do not default to em dashes. Parentheses are often a strong alternative and should be considered whenever they better fit the voice and tone of the passage.

Em dashes remain fully available when they create a meaningful interruption, emphasis, or rhythm that alternative punctuation cannot match. They are particularly effective when an aside is woven directly into the flow of a sentence.

Example:

She smiled—a little too quickly to be convincing—and looked away.

The goal is not to eliminate em dashes, but to preserve their impact. They should feel intentional rather than automatic, and exceptional rather than routine.
